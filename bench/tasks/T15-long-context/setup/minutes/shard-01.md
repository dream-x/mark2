### 1. Opening and purpose of the cycle

Dana opened the meeting at 10:00 and welcomed members to the FY2027 budget
cycle. She reminded the committee that it will meet six times between now and
the end of November, and that these minutes, together with the minutes of the
five meetings that follow, are the formal record of how each departmental
budget for FY2027 is arrived at. The committee approves departmental budgets.
The board, represented by Harold, reviews the result at the sixth meeting and
may adjust any figure; a board adjustment replaces whatever the committee
approved before it.

Six departments submit budgets in this cycle: Engineering, Marketing, Sales,
Customer Support, Facilities and People Operations. There are no other
departmental budgets. The running costs of Finance itself are absorbed into
the corporate overhead allocation and are not reviewed by the committee.

Dana set out the timetable. Engineering and Facilities are taken today, with a
first look at People Operations. Marketing and Sales follow on 22 September.
The infrastructure review, the return of Engineering and the Customer Support
request are scheduled for 6 October. People Operations returns on 20 October,
once the benefits renewal is known. The meeting on 3 November is reserved for
open items, and the board ratifies the budget on 17 November. She stressed
that any department's figure can be revisited at a later meeting if new
information arrives, and asked Julia to make sure the minutes always say
plainly when an earlier figure has been replaced.

### 2. Planning assumptions and comparison base

Ravi presented the planning assumptions every department was asked to use. The
merit pool for salary increases is 3.5 percent, effective 1 April 2027.
Employer social costs are budgeted at 14.1 percent of gross salary. The
planning exchange rates are 1.09 dollars to the euro and 1.27 dollars to the
pound; departments with supplier contracts in other currencies should convert
at those rates and should not add a hedging margin of their own. Travel is
capped at the FY2026 level per head, which works out at about $6,200 per
traveller per year.

For comparison, Ravi tabled the budgets approved for the current year, FY2026:

- Engineering: $3,980,000
- Marketing: $2,100,000
- Sales: $1,870,000
- Customer Support: $960,000
- Facilities: $730,000
- People Operations: $1,090,000

Together these came to $10,730,000. The year-end forecast of actual FY2026
spend is $10,544,000, an underspend of $186,000 that comes mostly from late
hiring in Engineering and Sales. Ravi cautioned members against reading the
underspend as slack: most of it is timing, and the people hired late will cost
a full year in FY2027.

Dana said the board has signalled an envelope of roughly $11,600,000 for
FY2027 departmental spending. She was clear that the envelope is a ceiling for
planning conversations, not an allocation, and that no department should treat
any share of it as its budget. Harold added that the board would rather see
the envelope undershot than met by default, and that its view of the cloud
contract renegotiation would shape its appetite for any increase in
Engineering.

A separate capital plan, covering the laptop refresh, the lab test rigs and
the Leeds meeting-room fit-out, is handled by the capital expenditure group
and currently totals $640,000. It is not part of any departmental budget and
is not counted in departmental totals. Likewise, sales commissions are paid
from a central variable-pay pool, forecast at about $410,000 for FY2027, which
sits outside the departmental budgets.

### 3. Engineering

Ingrid presented the Engineering request for FY2027: $4,350,000, compared with
$3,980,000 approved for FY2026 and a forecast FY2026 actual of $3,912,000. The
request breaks down as follows:

- Personnel: $3,050,000, covering 31 permanent staff today plus four planned
  hires, two of them in the platform team in the first half of the year.
- Cloud hosting: $860,000, assuming the current contract terms roll over
  unchanged.
- Tooling and licences: $280,000, of which roughly $95,000 is the new
  observability suite.
- Contractors: $160,000, mainly for the payments-integration work that cannot
  wait for the new hires.

Ingrid said the hosting line was the least certain part of the request. The
current contract with the cloud provider expires at the end of December, and
the infrastructure team has started a review of reserved capacity, storage
tiers and data egress. Early indications are that savings are possible, but
she would not commit to a number before the review finishes.

Dana questioned the four hires. Two FY2026 hires were made only this summer,
so their full-year cost is arriving for the first time and makes the personnel
line look steeper than the underlying growth. Ingrid accepted that framing but
said the platform hires are needed to retire the legacy job scheduler, which
caused two of the three customer-facing incidents this year. Priya supported
this from the support side: each of those incidents generated more than 400
tickets in a single day, and one of them took eleven days to clear.

Gwen asked whether the contractor line could shrink if the payments
integration slipped into FY2028. Ingrid said a slip was unlikely, because two
enterprise customers have the integration written into their renewals. Ravi
asked whether the observability suite could be paid annually instead of up
front; Ingrid will check, though she noted the vendor's annual price is about
8 percent higher.

The committee did not approve an Engineering figure today. The request stands
as submitted, for discussion only, and Engineering will be taken again at the
third meeting, after the infrastructure review has reported.

### 4. Facilities

Leon presented the Facilities request: $812,000, compared with $730,000
approved for FY2026. The increase is driven almost entirely by the planned
relocation of the warehouse annex in Leeds, budgeted at $140,000 for removals,
re-racking and a three-month overlap of two leases. Without the move the
request would have been noticeably below last year, mainly because the
cleaning contract was re-tendered in the summer at a lower rate.

Members asked why the annex had to move at all. Leon explained that the
landlord had refused to extend the lease beyond March 2027, and that the
alternative site was the only one within reasonable distance of the dispatch
team. He had made the landlord a final offer the previous week and expected an
answer within days. Tomas asked whether the annex could be given up
altogether, with stock held by the logistics partner; Leon said the partner
had quoted $9 per pallet per week, which would cost more than either lease
within eighteen months.

The item was paused while the committee took the People Operations preview,
and resumed later in the meeting (item 6).

### 5. People Operations (preview)

Amara gave a first view of the People Operations budget, which carries the
company-wide benefits administration fees, recruiting, and the learning and
development programme. FY2026 was approved at $1,090,000. The benefits renewal
is the dominant unknown, and the broker will not return the renewal quote
until mid-October. Until then People Operations has pencilled in $1,150,000
for FY2027 as a placeholder. Amara emphasised that this is a planning
placeholder only and that she will bring a proper request to the fourth
meeting, once the quote is in.

Dana agreed to carry the placeholder in the working model and asked that
nobody quote it as an approved number. No People Operations figure was
approved.

Members also discussed the recruiting load. Engineering's four hires and the
additions Sales is expected to propose would together need about six new
requisitions if all were approved, on top of the usual replacements, compared
with four new requisitions in FY2026. Amara said the in-house recruiting team
could absorb that volume without agency fees, which would otherwise run at
about 20 percent of first-year salary.

### 6. Facilities (resumed)

At 11:40 Leon reported that the landlord of the Leeds annex had replied during
the meeting and accepted a two-year lease extension. The relocation is
therefore cancelled, and the $140,000 for the move comes out of the request.
The extension is not free: the new rent is higher, which adds $22,000 for the
year. Leon confirmed that nothing else in the request changes.

With the move off the table, the committee signed off on a Facilities budget
for FY2027 of $694,000. Leon observed that it is the first year in five that
Facilities will come in below the previous year's approved figure.

Ravi asked whether the resurfacing of the Leeds car park, already deferred
twice, was included. Leon said it was not, and that he did not intend to raise
it in this cycle.

### 7. Process points

Ravi reminded members that all submissions must use version 4 of the template
released in August, with separate lines for personnel, external spend and
allocations. Two departments had used version 3, which buries allocations
inside external spend and makes year-on-year comparisons misleading. Julia
will recirculate the template.

The committee agreed a convention for these minutes: requests, placeholders,
working figures and envelope numbers are recorded for reference, but they are
not approvals. A figure becomes a department's approved budget only when the
minutes record a decision by the committee, or later by the board, to adopt
it.

### 8. Any other business

Gwen asked whether the Sales submission should include the cost of the annual
sales conference. Dana confirmed that, as in previous years, the conference is
charged to Sales and should be in its request. Priya asked for the
incident-cost analysis from the summer to be shared with all members; Ingrid
agreed to present it at the third meeting.

The meeting closed at 12:20.
