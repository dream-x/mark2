# Kestrel Ridge Software -- FY2027 Budget Committee

## Minutes of meeting 6 of 6 (board ratification)

- Date: Tuesday 17 November 2026, 14:00 to 16:10
- Place: Boardroom A, Bergen office
- Chair: Dana Whitlock, Chief Financial Officer
- Present: Ravi Menon, Ingrid Solberg, Tomas Ferreira, Gwen Achterberg, Priya
  Raman, Leon Brandt, Amara Okafor
- For the board: Harold Pike (board liaison) and Sigrid Lunde (chair of the
  audit and risk committee)
- Minutes: Julia Novak

### 1. Opening

Dana opened the final meeting of the cycle and welcomed Sigrid, who was
attending for the first time in this cycle and had reviewed the ratification
pack on the board's behalf in the week before the meeting. Dana then handed
over to Harold, who presented the board's decisions on the ratification pack.
Harold thanked the committee for a pack that was, in the board's words, the
clearest in years, and in particular for the reconciliation to FY2026, which
let the board see at a glance which changes were timing and which were real
growth. The board had also found the column of original requests useful,
although it stressed that the column has no standing and that it had looked at
it only to understand how each department's figure had moved.

### 2. Security audit and Engineering

The board considered the separate paper on the Halden Freight security audit.
The audit firm's indicative quote in October was $92,000; after the scope was
narrowed to the customer-facing platform, excluding the internal tools, the
final quote is $74,800. Sigrid explained the board's reasoning. The renewal is
worth considerably more than the audit; other enterprise customers are likely
to ask for the same evidence within a year; and an audit commissioned by
Kestrel Ridge itself can be reused, whereas one paid for by the customer would
belong to the customer. The board therefore decided that the company will pay
for the audit, and that the cost belongs in Engineering, which owns the
platform and any remediation work that follows.

Ingrid asked the board to also consider an additional $120,000 for a second
GPU cluster for the analytics team, which had not been part of the September
request. Harold said the board had seen her note but was not willing to add a
new item at ratification; she may bring it to the April reforecast or to the
FY2028 cycle. That request was declined.

The board settled Engineering's FY2027 budget at the figure the committee
approved at the third meeting plus the $74,800 for the audit, and nothing
else. The board's figure supersedes the committee's. The audit is planned for
the second quarter, so the second-quarter phasing rises by the same amount and
the other three quarters are unchanged.

Ingrid accepted the decision and confirmed that the audit firm can start in
April. Mateo will be the point of contact, and any remediation work identified
by the audit will be prioritised within the existing Engineering team rather
than through new contractors.

### 3. Ratification of the other departments

The board reviewed the remaining five departments against the minutes of the
earlier meetings. Sigrid asked about two items in particular: the deferral of
the Nordics sales hire, and the dropping of the chatbot pilot in Customer
Support. She was satisfied with both explanations. She also asked why
Marketing's final figure had come in well below the $2,640,000 originally
requested; Tomas summarised the trade-show decision and the trimmed campaign,
and Sigrid said the board welcomed the discipline.

Sigrid also asked whether the People Operations figure would hold if the
in-house recruiters needed agency help. Amara repeated her undertaking from
the fourth meeting to absorb any agency fee within the approved figure.

The board ratified every other departmental budget exactly as the committee
last approved it, without change. Harold confirmed that the board made no
adjustment to Marketing, Sales, Customer Support, Facilities or People
Operations. He added that the board had checked each figure in the pack
against the decision recorded in the minutes of the earlier meetings and had
found no discrepancies.

### 4. Items outside the departmental budgets

For completeness the board also noted two items that are not departmental
budgets and are not part of any departmental total:

- The capital plan, which has risen from $640,000 to $678,000 now that the
  capital expenditure group has accepted the Leeds car park resurfacing.
- The central variable-pay pool for sales commissions, now forecast at
  $425,000 for FY2027 after the revised quota plan, compared with the $410,000
  mentioned at the first meeting.

### 5. Risks noted by the board

Sigrid presented the risks that the audit and risk committee wants watched
during FY2027:

- Hosting: the one-year cloud agreement means the hosting price will be
  renegotiated again in late 2027; a return to the old terms would cost
  Engineering about $245,000 a year.
- Hiring: several of the approved positions start in the first quarter; delays
  would produce another underspend, which the board does not want to see
  recycled into unbudgeted items.
- Support response times: the board asked for the first-response measure to be
  reported monthly, not only at the April reforecast.
- Currency: the planning rates of 1.09 dollars to the euro and 1.27 dollars to
  the pound will be reviewed at the reforecast, with no change to departmental
  figures unless a movement exceeds 5 percent.

Harold added that the board would look at the hosting risk again in September
2027, before the provider makes its next offer, and asked Ingrid to start the
next infrastructure review in June rather than August.

Sigrid also asked that the risk register record the dependency between the
Support response-time target and the routing work in Engineering's
internal-tools team, since a delay in one would show up in the other. Ingrid
and Priya agreed to report on it jointly. Leon asked whether the Leeds annex
extension belonged on the register; Sigrid said it did not, since the lease
now runs to March 2029, but that it should be added two years from now.

### 6. Board questions on the FY2026 outturn

Sigrid asked about the FY2026 underspend reported at the first meeting. Ravi
said the year-end forecast has moved slightly since September, mainly because
of the Las Vegas deposit refund agreed in early November, but that the picture
is unchanged: most of the underspend is the timing of hires in Engineering and
Sales, and none of it has been carried forward into FY2027. Sigrid asked that
the FY2027 monthly reports make it easy to see whether the same pattern
repeats, which the new headcount line should do.

Harold asked whether any department expected to overspend in the remaining
weeks of FY2026. Priya said Support would finish slightly above its FY2026
budget because of the spring incidents, as reported at the third meeting; no
other department expects an overspend.

### 7. Communication to staff

Amara and Dana agreed how the outcome of the cycle will be communicated.
Department heads will brief their own teams in the first week of December on
what the budget means for them: which hires go ahead, which projects are
funded and which have been deferred. Dana will present the overall picture at
the all-hands meeting on 3 December in terms of priorities rather than
amounts; departmental figures are shared with department heads and the board
only. Gwen asked that the deferred Nordics hire be explained carefully, since
the inside-sales representatives involved in the test may read it as a
judgement on the territory. Amara will prepare a short note for managers.
Tomas asked whether the trade-show programme could be mentioned at the
all-hands, since several teams outside Marketing help to staff the stands;
Dana agreed to mention the two confirmed shows and to thank the volunteers
from last year.

### 8. Closing the cycle

With ratification complete, Dana declared the FY2027 departmental budgets
closed. The approved figures are those recorded in these minutes and in the
minutes of the five earlier meetings; Ravi will publish them in the FY2027
budget book on 1 December. Any change from now on requires the formal in-year
reforecast, the first of which is scheduled for April 2027, and no department
may move money between budgets without the CFO's written approval.

Dana thanked members, Harold and Sigrid, and Julia for the minutes. Members
held a short lessons-learned discussion. The template change worked. The
placeholder convention for People Operations was useful and should become
standard for any line that depends on an outside quote. Deferring decisions
that depended on outside information, such as the trade-show calendar, the
benefits quote, the backlog analysis and the infrastructure review, saved
several rounds of revision. Gwen suggested that next year's cycle start two
weeks earlier, so that the Marketing decision does not have to wait for the
trade-show calendar until the fifth meeting; Dana agreed to consider it. Priya
asked that the incident-cost analysis become a standing paper in every cycle,
and members agreed.

The meeting closed at 16:10.
